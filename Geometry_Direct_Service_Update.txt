Geometry Update for Direct Service and Ablation
Service projection extension version 1
Anonymous · CC0 1.0 Universal · 9 September 2026
The geometry gains a service layer. The retained representation can preserve execution stages, provenance, corrections, and dependencies while the served interface presents the user object and its answer directly. This extends the existing witness-relative quotient and descent framework. Its core definitions, predictive results, and domain-specific mathematics survive.
The central distinction is internal necessity ≠ visible necessity. A stage can matter to an internal process without needing to appear as a separate conversational object. The ablation evidence establishes a concrete presentation transformation: separately retained records can be removed while selected recorded answer text remains exactly unchanged. A complete direct-service repair also needs a correct answer-selection rule, task completion, and behavioral correction binding.
This release supplies the five proposed additions, proves their relation to the existing geometry, and places them in the relevant source families. References S001–S123 identify the reviewed Drive/Docs sources; E1–E4 identify the ablation evidence. The source register distinguishes editions and preserves their lineage.

7 Present service and future state
The predictive geometry asks whether a retained state determines its successor. The service quotient asks whether a current answer can be presented without separate interposition. These are different obligations.

Current-answer equality does not imply this condition. As a formal counterexample, let two internal records serve the same answer now but carry correction bits 0 and 1. Define their next answer payloads to be 0 and 1 respectively under the same input. Their current service labels agree and their next direct-service labels differ. This is a constructed counterexample to the implication, not a finding about a hidden runtime bit.
The architecture can therefore minimize displayed stages while retaining a richer state for future response control.
