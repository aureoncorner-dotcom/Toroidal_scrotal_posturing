1 Kind VI — Spectral witness family
“VI” is a bookkeeping label for spectral witnesses, not a new exception to descent logic or a sixth disjoint defect species. It can overlap the diagnostic patterns in Typed Defects; the tested domain may consist of operators or of whole operator paths.
Eligibility. Specify a family x↦A_x (or x↦γ_x) on a domain D and a well-defined W:D→Y. For eta, a standard sufficient setting is a self-adjoint elliptic operator on a closed compact manifold with discrete spectrum and eta continuation regular at zero. Self-adjointness or Fredholmness alone does not ensure these properties. Set η(A)=η_A(0), h=dim ker A, and ξ=(η+h)/2; declare whether ξ is real-valued or reduced modulo ℤ [R1].
Repair. Retain data proved sufficient for the chosen functional, or use the attained refinement S_W(π)=im(π,W). Signed eigenvalues, multiplicities, zero modes, and analytic continuation or certified remainder bounds matter for eta. A finite spectral approximation has no automatic exact guarantee. For spectral flow retain a sufficient path invariant, or its value; adding W is a set-level repair, not a computational or predictive sufficiency claim.
Unsupported shortcuts.
* Replacing eta by an ordinary local Seeley–DeWitt density or by GHY. Eta can be represented through a regularized signed heat trace, so “global” does not mean unrelated to heat analysis [R1].
* Excluding spectral flow from fiber comparisons. A path is a valid point of D; two paths can share π and have different spectral flow. It can also satisfy pattern I when a retained relation and lost output are specified.
* Equating eta with toroidal winding, a 39-screen phase, or a service answer without an explicit correspondence.
* Identifying APS spectral boundary conditions with a pointwise local boundary projector; their realizations and zero-mode conventions must be specified [R1,R3].
Relation to V. A representation gap uses j:A→Z and an explicit z∈Z\j(A), as in corrected Typed Defects §16.2. It is separate from an attained-label collision. Spectral witnesses need not occur on a boundary. If the operator/path or definition is missing, the proposed spectral test is not ready to classify.
Certification. A spectral non-descent claim still requires π(x)=π(x′) and W(x)≠W(x′), or an equivalent proof of failure to factor through π. A computed nonzero eta or spectral flow alone establishes only a value. If π retains W, even nonzero W descends. Certified numerical intervals may establish separation; overlapping bounds or missing computations leave the claim UNRESOLVED.
1.1 E1 — Equal squared spectra, different eta
On H=L²(S¹;ℂ), θ modulo 2π, take the common periodic H¹ domain and A_a=−i∂θ+a, 0<a<1. The eigenfunctions e^(inθ) have eigenvalues n+a, n∈ℤ. Let D_op={A_a:0<a<1}, π(A)=Spec(A²) as an unordered multiset with multiplicities, and W(A)=η(A).
For Re(s)>1, η_Aa(s)=ζ(s,a)−ζ(s,1−a). Meromorphic continuation and ζ(0,a)=1/2−a give η(A_a)=1−2a [R0]. Since h=0 here, ξ(A_a)=1/2−a as a real value.
Take a=1/4 and b=3/4. Reindexing n↦−n−1 gives n+3/4=−[(−n−1)+1/4]; their squared spectra are exactly the same, including multiplicities. Hence Tr exp(−tA²) agrees for every t>0, while η(A_1/4)=1/2 and η(A_3/4)=−1/2; ξ equals 1/4 and −1/4 respectively.
This is a located same-label pair with eta separation 1, so π(A_1/4)∈N_η(π). With ordinary real distance it certifies any threshold ε<1. Equality of the complete unsigned heat trace is stronger than equality of any finite list of its heat coefficients. It still does not retain signed spectral asymmetry. This example does not assert equality of the operator-valued heat kernels.
1.2 E2 — Equal endpoint spectra, different spectral flow
Use the same Hilbert space and common domain, now allowing real a. On D_path={γ₀,γ₁}, define γ₀(t)=A_(−1/2) and γ₁(t)=A_(t−1/2), 0≤t≤1. These are continuous self-adjoint Fredholm paths with compact resolvent and invertible endpoints.
Retain π(γ)=(Spec γ(0),Spec γ(1)), the ordered pair of endpoint spectral multisets. Both labels are (ℤ+1/2,ℤ+1/2), because ℤ−1/2=ℤ+1/2. The endpoint operators of the two paths are not identical; only the retained spectra agree.
The constant path has sf(γ₀)=0. Along γ₁, eigenvalues are n+t−1/2. Only n=0 crosses zero in (0,1), at t=1/2, and its derivative is +1. Thus sf(γ₁)=+1 under the stated orientation [R2]. This is an exact same-fiber collision on a path domain, with separation 1.
Neither example identifies an analytical operator with the native toroidal kernel. They demonstrate how a properly declared foreign witness enters the existing non-descent calculus. Retaining W repairs that W; another output or a future law needs its own test.

3 Three distinctions that govern imports
U1 — Heat densities can be witnesses on a declared domain
For a declared Laplace-type P on a geometric domain, interior heat coefficients depend locally on jets of its geometric data. Distinguish the pointwise density a_j(x;P), its integral, and the full heat kernel or trace. With P=−(connection Laplacian+E), the interior a₂ density is (4π)^(−m/2) tr(E+R·Id/6) dvol in dimension m [R11].
To use a density as W:D→Y, declare d↦P_d and an evaluation/integration rule; comparing density fields also needs identified domains and a codomain. Thus heat densities can be witnesses. Their locality does not establish constancy on any chosen observation fiber.
Neither the formula for a₂ nor a displayed heat expansion proves descent through π_DS or π_q. Test the resulting W against that π. Example E1 in §1.1 shows that even the complete unsigned heat trace can lose eta.
U2 — Local boundary terms and APS eta have different definitions
Choose an admissible elliptic realization (P,B), with self-adjointness when required. Dirichlet, Robin, and mixed conditions for Laplace-type P have specific hypotheses; an arbitrary pointwise spinor projector does not automatically define a self-adjoint elliptic Dirac problem. APS instead uses a spectral projection of a tangential operator, with orientation, sign, and zero-mode conventions [R1,R3].
Boundary heat coefficients and the GHY integral are local boundary expressions under their stated assumptions. Eta measures signed spectral asymmetry. Chamseddine–Connes obtain the Einstein–Hilbert/GHY relative boundary coefficient for their particular operator and local mixed boundary conditions; that result does not replace them by APS or identify GHY with eta [R3].
These objects may be related by index or variation formulas after their hypotheses are supplied; they are not interchangeable names. Neither a boundary coefficient nor eta alone proves a missing-data defect. The existing service deletion-cost non-descent result keeps its own domain and witness.
U3 — Predictive autonomy and spectral flow test different data
D5: for deterministic U:D→D, a set-level autonomous retained update exists iff N_(π∘U)(π)=∅. For stochastic dynamics, equal next-observation laws on fibers test universal law-level closure; measurable reduced kernels require the appropriate measurability assumptions.
Spectral flow is an integer-valued invariant of a continuous path of self-adjoint Fredholm operators in a specified admissible topology. Fix crossing orientation and endpoint conventions; negative-to-positive crossings count positively here. It does not generally require eta-style zeta regularization [R2].
A relation between D5 and spectral flow needs a map connecting the dynamical states and operator paths, plus a compatibility theorem. None is supplied here, so neither an sf=0 claim nor a predictive-closure claim can stand in for the other. The Sophie Germain clock remains a D5 example.
