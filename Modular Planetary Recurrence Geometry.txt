Modular Planetary Recurrence Geometry


A Witness-Relative Circuit Framework for Geocentric Angular Configurations


Working draft v0.1 — exploratory methods paper


Abstract


Planetary “alignment” is usually treated as an informal visual category. This paper replaces that category with a reproducible geometry of labelled angular circuits.


For \(n\) bodies represented by geocentric ecliptic longitudes, the instantaneous planetary state is naturally an element of the \(n\)-torus


\[
\mathbb T^n=(\mathbb R/360^\circ\mathbb Z)^n.
\]


Because a common angular rotation of every longitude leaves all relative planetary geometry unchanged, the physically relevant relative-longitude configuration lies in the quotient


\[
\boxed{
\mathbb T^n/\mathbb T_{\Delta}\cong\mathbb T^{n-1}
}
\]


where \(\mathbb T_\Delta\) is the diagonal global-rotation action.


Within this quotient space, different observational witnesses retain different information. A full pairwise-distance witness, a signed-orientation witness, a midpoint witness, a lattice witness, and a selected three-body circuit witness generally induce different notions of recurrence. This distinction is not cosmetic: exploratory analysis of planetary states from 1775 through 2026 repeatedly found that whole configurations rarely recur closely while small body-labelled modules can recur with much lower residual error.


The recurrent primitives observed in the discovery corpus include conjunction or double nodes, midpoint chains, \(30^\circ\)-family triads, opposition gates, nested angular rails, and fine common lattices such as the \(7.5^\circ\) structure observed on 10 March 2000.


The principal methodological result is therefore not an asserted connection between planetary geometry and historical events. It is the construction of a testable object:


\[
\boxed{
\text{planetary state}
\rightarrow
\text{declared witness}
\rightarrow
\text{circuit decomposition}
\rightarrow
\text{recurrence score}
}
\]


Historical labels can subsequently be compared with peaks generated by the frozen geometric system. They must not determine the geometry used to find those peaks.

1. The missing object


A list of planetary longitudes is not yet a geometry.


Likewise, statements such as “six planets were aligned,” “three planets formed a triangle,” or “the sky resembled an earlier date” leave unspecified what distinctions are being preserved.


The first requirement is therefore to declare the observational architecture.


This requirement closely parallels the retained-context formulation developed in GQG: an observational specification records not merely a resulting quotient, but the witness, retained context or conditioning, and realization by which the result was produced. 


The same separation is made explicitly in the companion Hidden Quotient construction: the quotient records distinctions annihilated by the witness, while the faithful realization records how surviving classes act; retained context is a refinement rather than something that can be silently presumed to survive the quotient. 


For planetary geometry, this means that the following are different questions:


\[
\text{Are these two complete skies similar?}
\]


\[
\text{Do they contain the same labelled triad?}
\]


\[
\text{Do the planets occupy the same distances from a hinge?}
\]


\[
\text{Do they have the same orientation around that hinge?}
\]


\[
\text{Do several independently defined modules occur simultaneously?}
\]


An answer to one does not silently answer the others.

2. Planetary configuration space is literally toroidal


Let


\[
\lambda_i(t)\in\mathbb T
\]


be the geocentric ecliptic longitude of body \(i\).


Then an \(n\)-body state is


\[
\Lambda(t)
=
(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)
\in\mathbb T^n.
\]


This already gives a mathematically literal toroidal state space before any proposed electromagnetic interpretation is introduced.


A global angular displacement


\[
\lambda_i\mapsto\lambda_i+\alpha
\]


for every planet does not alter any relative angular relation. Therefore the diagonal circle action can be removed:


\[
\Lambda
\sim
\Lambda+\alpha(1,\ldots,1).
\]


Thus the relative state belongs to


\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_n
=
\mathbb T^n/\mathbb T_\Delta
\cong
\mathbb T^{\,n-1}.
}
\]


Choosing Pluto, Jupiter, Neptune, Saturn, or any other planet as the displayed \(0^\circ\) node is therefore only a coordinate choice on the quotient.


It does not make that body mathematically privileged.


This explains why the “Architect views” developed during the exploratory work were useful: setting a body to zero exposed the relative toroidal coordinates without changing the underlying configuration.

3. Signed and unsigned geometry


Define the signed circular difference


\[
\delta_{ij}
=
\operatorname{wrap}_{(-180^\circ,180^\circ]}
(\lambda_i-\lambda_j).
\]


The unsigned separation is


\[
d_{ij}=|\delta_{ij}|.
\]


These are different witnesses.


The unsigned witness identifies a configuration with its mirror image:


\[
\delta_{ij}\longleftrightarrow-\delta_{ij}.
\]


The signed witness does not.


Consequently,


\[
\boxed{
\text{same shells}
\neq
\text{necessarily same directed circuit}.
}
\]


This became visible during the September 11, 2021 analysis, where an older configuration could reproduce the approximate Pluto-centered radial shells while placing one body on the opposite side of the reference seam.


Under the unsigned witness that can be a close recurrence.


Under the directed witness it is not.


GQG provides exactly the right rule for this distinction: a finer retained witness induces a finer equivalence, and information removed by a coarser witness cannot be silently restored afterward.

4. Circuit primitives


The exploratory corpus repeatedly produced a small family of geometric primitives.


They can now be defined independently of any historical date.


Double node


Bodies \(i,j\) form a double node when


\[
r_0(i,j)=d_{ij}
\]


is small.


The tolerance must be frozen before confirmatory use.




---


Opposition gate


\[
r_{180}(i,j)
=
|d_{ij}-180^\circ|.
\]


A low residual identifies a near diameter.




---


Target-angle edge


For a declared angle \(\theta\),


\[
r_\theta(i,j)
=
|d_{ij}-\theta|.
\]


The discovery corpus repeatedly exposed the family


\[
30^\circ,\ 60^\circ,\ 90^\circ,\ 120^\circ,\ 150^\circ,\ 180^\circ.
\]


The observation itself does not give those numbers causal status; it makes them a candidate frozen vocabulary for the next experiment.




---


Midpoint operator


Body \(j\) lies at the circular midpoint of \(i\) and \(k\) when


\[
2\lambda_j
=
\lambda_i+\lambda_k
\pmod{360^\circ}.
\]


Define midpoint displacement


\[
r_{\rm mid}(i,j,k)
=
\frac12
\left|
\operatorname{wrap}
(2\lambda_j-\lambda_i-\lambda_k)
\right|.
\]


December 11, 1978 was especially striking because several distinct triples simultaneously produced very small midpoint residuals.




---


Angular lattice


Choose reference body \(r\) and spacing \(\Delta\).


For each retained body \(i\),


\[
\delta_{ir}
\approx
k_i\Delta,
\qquad
k_i\in\mathbb Z.
\]


A lattice score is


\[
D_\Delta^2
=
\frac1m
\sum_i
\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}
\left|
\operatorname{wrap}
(\delta_{ir}-k\Delta)
\right|^2.
\]


March 10, 2000 produced the strongest example encountered during discovery: with Neptune chosen as the displayed origin, the major planets approximated


\[
\boxed{
-52.5,\ 0,\ 15,\ 22.5,\ 30,\ 75,\ 90,\ 97.5^\circ
}
\]


on a common


\[
\boxed{\Delta=7.5^\circ}
\]


grid.


This is not merely a collection of unrelated pairwise aspects. It is one shared quantized coordinate description.

5. A module is a labelled graph


A planetary circuit module is defined as


\[
\boxed{
C=(B,E,\Theta,R)
}
\]


where:


\(B\) specifies the body labels, \(E\) specifies which relations are tested, \(\Theta\) contains target angles or midpoint constraints, and \(R\) specifies retained information such as handedness.


For a module containing \(m\) constraints, define


\[
\boxed{
D_C(t)
=
\sqrt{
\frac1m
\sum_{e=1}^{m}
r_e(t)^2
}.
}
\]


A small \(D_C\) means that the body-labelled module at time \(t\) resembles its frozen target.


This is substantially stricter than saying that the sky “looks similar.”


It allows statements such as:


> this three-body circuit has a residual of \(0.4^\circ\),






while simultaneously allowing:


> the complete eight-body configuration is not a recurrence.






Those two statements can coexist without contradiction because they are produced by different declared witnesses.

7. The strongest discovery


The most persistent finding across the exploratory runs was:


\[
\boxed{
\textbf{whole skies rarely recur closely; modules often do.}
}
\]


That distinction changes the model.


If planetary geometry were simply periodic repetition of entire configurations, one would expect the full pairwise matrix to produce strong historical clones.


Instead, exploratory backward scans repeatedly behaved more like:


\[
C_1
\]


reappearing on one recurrence clock,


\[
C_2
\]


on another,


and occasionally


\[
C_1\land C_2\land C_3
\]


appearing together.


The architecture therefore resembles modular superposition more than whole-state repetition.


Schematically,


\[
\boxed{
\text{slow module}
+
\text{intermediate module}
+
\text{fast/transient module}
=
\text{instantaneous assembly}.
}
\]


That is exactly what one would expect mathematically from bodies possessing widely different orbital periods.


The nontrivial question is whether some combinations occur more often, more tightly, or at historically interesting times than expected under an appropriately constructed null model.

8. Triadic overlap


A useful primitive is the three-body module.


Let


\[
C_{ijk}
\]


contain the three pairwise angular separations among planets \(i,j,k\).


A triadic target such as


\[
30^\circ,\ 60^\circ,\ 90^\circ
\]


has score


\[
D_{ijk}
=
\sqrt{
\frac{
r_{30}^2+r_{60}^2+r_{90}^2
}{3}
}.
\]


Multiple triads can share vertices.


Thus a state may have


\[
C_A=(i,j,k),
\]


\[
C_B=(k,l,m),
\]


so that body \(k\) serves as a routing vertex.


This is what “triadic overlap” can mean mathematically without requiring metaphor:


\[
\boxed{
\text{two labelled three-body subgraphs sharing one or more vertices}.
}
\]


The 1918 May window was one especially obvious discovery example: two differently labelled triads simultaneously approached simple angular families.

9. Circuit overlap density


Let the frozen module library be


\[
\mathcal L=
\{C_1,\ldots,C_N\}.
\]


A simple descriptive activation function is


\[
A_i(t)
=
\exp
\left[
-\frac{D_{C_i}(t)^2}{2\sigma_i^2}
\right].
\]


A total circuit-density trace can then be drawn:


\[
\boxed{
\Omega(t)=
\sum_i w_i A_i(t).
}
\]


Large \(\Omega(t)\) means several modules are simultaneously close to their frozen geometries.


However, this must not automatically be interpreted as statistical evidence, because many modules share planets or edges and therefore are not independent.


GQG makes exactly this distinction: separate witness claims may be conjoined without silently declaring them independent; promotion to a stronger conclusion requires an explicit rule. 


Therefore \(\Omega(t)\) is initially a descriptive overlap statistic.


Its null distribution must be measured.

11. Time-freeze rule


The discovery work used several kinds of timestamps: documented event times, neutral noon freezes, astronomical anchors such as a Full Moon, and occasionally an optimized geometric instant.


Those are useful during exploration but cannot remain mixed during confirmation.


The confirmatory protocol should use:


\[
t=
\begin{cases}
\text{documented event timestamp}, & \text{if known before geometry analysis},\\[2mm]
12{:}00\ {\rm UTC}, & \text{if only a date is known}.
\end{cases}
\]


A geometric optimum inside a date may still be calculated, but it must be labelled a different observable:


\[
t_{\rm geom}
=
\arg\min_tD_C(t).
\]


It cannot be substituted for the historical timestamp.


This preserves the distinction between:


\[
\boxed{\text{event-time geometry}}
\]


and


\[
\boxed{\text{best geometry occurring somewhere that day}}.
\]

12. What would falsify the useful version?


This framework becomes uninteresting if the module language is so flexible that virtually every date can be represented as an impressive circuit.


That can be tested.


The useful hypothesis fails if, after freezing the vocabulary:


1. comparable low module scores occur nearly everywhere;




2. historical target dates are not enriched near high-overlap states relative to matched random dates;




3. apparent recurrences disappear when the same body labels and orientation are required;




4. results depend strongly on small post hoc changes in target angles or tolerances;




5. holdout data fail while discovery dates continue to look impressive;




6. a simpler null description explains the apparent structure equally well.






Those are not attacks on the architecture.


They are what make it capable of learning something.

The geometry remains stable even if the physical interpretation later changes.


That is a feature.

14. Why Hidden Quotient matters here


The Hidden Quotient core begins with the observation that a representation does not preserve every distinction in its source; one must identify exactly what the chosen witness can and cannot see. 


That lesson becomes concrete in this project.


A full-sky witness retains almost everything.


A module witness forgets the rest of the planets.


A radial-shell witness forgets handedness.


An unsigned aspect witness forgets orientation.


A lattice witness forgets deviations orthogonal to its chosen spacing.


Those different acts of forgetting generate different equivalence relations.


Therefore:


\[
\boxed{
\textbf{“recurs” is meaningless without specifying the witness under which it recurs.}
}
\]


That may be the most important sentence in the paper.


The v1.1 retained-context bridge makes the complementary point: if orientation, boundary choice, sector, normalization, or another distinction matters to a later claim, it has to be retained explicitly rather than assumed to have survived a coarser quotient. 


Planetary recurrence turns out to be an excellent worked example of that architecture.

The exploratory investigation began as a search for unusual planetary alignments around selected dates.


It produced something more useful.


The planetary longitude state is naturally toroidal. Removing global angular origin produces a relative toroidal configuration space. Within that space, different declared witnesses expose different recurrence structures.
